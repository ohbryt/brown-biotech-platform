### 1. [Signal 1 - Peptide and Protein-Design Lane + A Conserved LisH-Derived ~30-Residue Helix-Turn-Helix Motif Identified as a Class of Transcriptional Squelchers That Inhibit Transcription Indirectly by Redistributing Cofactors Away From DNA + a Small Set of Hydrophobic Residues Inside the First Helix Shown to Be Required for Effective Reporter Suppression + Binding to the General Transcriptional Cofactor Taf5 Identified as the Mechanistic Node, and Transcriptome-Level Effects Shown to Be Largely Shared Across Two Distinct LisH Sequences + Taf5 Overexpression Partially Reversing the Squelching Effect + Shortest Sequence YC5C-H1 Preferentially Squelching High-Expression Genes and Sharply Slowing Growth of the Human Colorectal Line HCT-15] GSE354717 'A conserved dimerization motif squelches transcription' (Saccharomyces cerevisiae; Homo sapiens, score 6, n=12 deposited libraries, pdat 2026/11/20, gds_type Expression profiling by high throughput sequencing, raw CSV, license NCBI public repository) is the peptide-lane pick of the day and the first archive appearance of a squelching-motif class. The mechanism is the commercially interesting half and it is unusual: squelching is an INDIRECT inhibition of transcription that results from redistribution of cofactors away from DNA, so a short peptide can silence a programme without touching a single promoter - which is precisely the property that separates this class from the DNA-binding peptides that dominate the archive's peptide lane, and it is the reason the authors chose a synthetic yeast reporter assay to find it. The screen design is a phylogenetically diverse subset of LisH domains, a ~30-amino-acid helix-turn-helix motif present in tens of thousands of proteins, which lets a 12-library dataset cover a huge sequence space rather than a handful of candidates. The residue-level result is what makes the record reusable: a small number of hydrophobic residues within the FIRST helix are required for effective inhibition of reporter expression, so the determinant is a position-specific sub-motif rather than the whole domain. The mechanistic node is named and validated twice: squelching required binding to the general transcriptional cofactor Taf5, a subunit of the TFIID complex; transcriptome-level impacts were largely correlated in yeast expressing two distinct LisH sequences, consistent with a shared mechanism of sequestering Taf5; and overexpression of Taf5 partially reversed the effect. The translational arm is stated explicitly as anti-cancer interest: the shortest squelching sequence, YC5C-H1, was tested in the human colorectal cancer cell line HCT-15, where it preferentially squelched high-expression genes and sharply slowed cell growth. Why it matters for BB: this is the cleanest peptide-service asset in the archive to date because it comes with a mechanism, a minimal binding motif, a rescue experiment, and a human-cell functional readout - the four things a peptide brief usually lacks. The commercially legible framing is that a ~30-residue, non-DNA-binding, cofactor-sequestering scaffold is a design space where selectivity can be tuned by a handful of first-helix hydrophobics, and the AI drug discovery service can own the enumeration and off-target-prediction work around the TFIID interface. The honest caveats are that the primary assay is synthetic yeast, the human data is one colorectal cell line, and the effect was partial even with Taf5 overexpression - this is a mechanism-plus-lead-stage record, not a validated therapeutic.

The framing is the commercially valuable part, and it is a change of framing rather than another variant catalogue: the standard genetic-risk story is that ultra-rare missense variants in UMOD cause highly penetrant autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease, whereas the more frequent T62P variant conveys INTERMEDIATE risk with VARIABLE penetrance - and this record asks the question almost nobody asks, namely whether that variability is explained by age and polygenic background. The cohort scale is what makes the answer credible: genotype and phenotype data from 882,306 individuals were combined across UK Biobank as discovery and All of Us and MyCode as validation cohorts, and the T62P association with higher CKD risk was reproducibly found, with the effect INCREASING WITH AGE. The interaction result is the headline: T62P carriers in the top GPS quintile have over 5-fold higher CKD risk versus the population average. Why it matters for BB: this is a ready-made template for the longevity lane's standing client question of how to stratify a common variant cohort into a risk segment that justifies a development programme - here the answer is a gene-by-age-by-polygenic triple stratification with an explicit odds ratio, and the cellular read converts a genetic observation into a targetable stress mechanism (ER retention in the tubular epithelium). It also hands the AI drug discovery service a target class - maturation and trafficking of uromodulin - with a graded, human-relevant disease axis.
