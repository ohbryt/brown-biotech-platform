## 🎯 Highlights

### 1. [Signal 1 - GSE322795 single-cell atlas of basal and learning-induced transcriptional dynamics underlying hippocampal aging and memory decline, Mus musculus, score 4, n=135 samples, pdat 2026/10/20 = FRESHEST NOVEL accession band of the day, raw RDS]: the longevity pick of the day and the first NOVEL learning-induced hippocampal-aging atlas ever published in this archive. The dataset is unusually large for a behaviourally-anchored single-nucleus design - approximately 0.63 million cells from the hippocampal formation - and, more importantly, it is indexed to the RESPONSIVENESS of the tissue rather than to its steady state: early (1 hour) and late (5 hour) learning-induced transcriptional programs are captured in both young mice and in aged mice stratified by moderate-to-severe memory deficit. The finding that matters is quantitative and layered: learning elicits discrete neuronal cell-state transitions and temporally distinct molecular programs; aging disrupts those programs in proportion to memory-impairment severity, with the damage concentrated in excitatory neurons, oligodendroglia and microglia; and beyond the memory-associated genes dysregulated at the basal state or upon learning, a substantial and previously unrecognised subset shows basal-state alterations that compromise its learning-induced responsiveness - i.e. the cells still look healthy until you ask them to do work. Genes are then ranked by correlation with memory performance and cell-cell interaction networks are inferred to nominate candidate molecular and intercellular pathways linking hippocampal aging to cognitive decline. Why it matters for BB: this is the closest thing the watcher's aging axis has produced to a functional-aging readout rather than a descriptive aging atlas - the deliverable a client can act on is a ranked susceptibility programme (which cell types fail to re-induce their learning program, and at what memory-score threshold), which maps directly onto BB's longevity lane promise of a measurable geroprotector-selection readout rather than an endpoint of drug exposure. It also supplies a design template: a stimulus-plus-timepoint single-nucleus design that separates 'cell is damaged' from 'cell can still respond', which is the same discrimination BB's peptide/geroprotector programme needs when scoring candidates in aged tissue.

At three mice per arm the dataset is small and must be treated as a hypothesis-generating state definition rather than a powered comparison, but the 10x OCM 3' format and the MTX + TSV manifest mean the state definitions can be re-derived and re-annotated against BB's own client tissue without licence friction, and the alveolar-macrophage state vocabulary it produces is directly reusable as a companion atlas for any human lung or fibrotic-tissue brief.
