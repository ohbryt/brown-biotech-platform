### 2. [Signal 2 - Refractory-Cancer Lane + a Two-Agent Combination with a Non-Redundant Mechanism in Aggressive Orthotopic PDAC + Interferon Signalling as the Shared Node and Macrophages as the Required Chassis] GSE312466 'SUMO Inhibition Plus CD40 Agonism Increases Anti-Tumor Immunogenicity Through Interferon Mediated Macrophage Activation [scRNA-seq]' (Mus musculus, score 4, n=8, pdat 2026/11/12, linked PMID 41638871) is the refractory-cancer pick of the day, and a good demonstration of why the score-4 band should not be skipped. The premise is a translation argument: SUMO inhibition has documented pro-immunogenic effects on myeloid cells, cancer cells and T cells in non-PDAC models, and it has recently been reported to act directly on PDAC; the question here is whether it can complement CD40 agonism in the hardest solid tumour rather than in a permissive model. The answer is yes, and the mechanism is specified: the combination of the small-molecule SUMO inhibitor TAK-981 with antibody-mediated CD40 agonism improves survival in an aggressive orthotopic mouse model of PDAC, amplifies CD8+ T-cell tumour infiltration, and induces significant changes among macrophages; TAK-981 also enhances cancer-specific MHC-I expression both in vitro and in vivo by augmenting interferon signalling; and the authors show the survival improvement is mediated by macrophages. Why it matters for BB: the commercially decisive feature is that this is a combination-design precedent with two named agents, a defined non-overlapping mechanism (SUMO-inhibitor-driven interferon signalling supplying tumour antigen presentation, CD40 agonism supplying the priming signal), a survival endpoint in an orthotopic aggressive model, and a mechanistic dependency (macrophages) that can be turned into a patient-selection biomarker. That is the exact argument structure a refractory-cancer brief has to make, and it is now available with a clinical candidate already in hand. The caution is that both agents are preclinical-stage combinations in mice and the interferon axis is a known toxicity liability in combination, so a client-facing brief should lead with design quality and treat the combination as a candidate regimen, not as a near-term clinical plan.
