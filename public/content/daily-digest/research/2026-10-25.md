Signal 1 (AI Drug Discovery Lane + Refractory-Cancer Lane + AR-Inhibition Resistance + TRIM24 Bi-Functional Degrader + Residual-Disease Adaptation + First NOVEL TRIM24-Degrader SuperSeries of the Day) - GSE267347 (SuperSeries, n=125) / GSE267345 (RNA-seq arm, n=21) / GSE267346 (scRNA-seq arm, n=17) 'TRIM24 degradation counteracts adaptation to androgen receptor inhibition in prostate cancer' (Homo sapiens, score 6, pdat 2026/10/23 = FRESHEST NOVEL accession of the day, raw_file_availability 'yes', license NCBI public repository [clean +2], query 'tumor metabolism single-cell'). KEY mechanistic content, read from the depositor abstract: androgen receptor (AR) signaling is the key lineage-specific oncogenic pathway and primary therapeutic target in prostate cancer, but responses to androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) and AR signaling inhibitors (ARSi) are highly variable and progression to fatal castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) is, in many cases, inevitable - driven by AR reactivation, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and lineage plasticity. Critically, LITTLE IS KNOWN ABOUT THE EARLY ADAPTATION MECHANISMS IN RESIDUAL DISEASE THAT INITIATE THESE PROCESSES. The authors identify TRIM24 as a critical regulator supporting survival of residual cancer-cell subpopulations primed for relapse IN VIVO, and show that degradation of TRIM24 through BI-FUNCTIONAL DEGRADERS (dTRIM24) significantly DELAYS OR ABROGATES the emergence of CRPC driven by AR reactivation and lineage plasticity. Mechanistically dTRIM24 inhibits AR/mTOR signaling in residual disease AND counteracts adaptive pathways directly engaged by AR inhibition, such as STAT3 signaling and the release of EMT-promoting cytokines; its activity extends to CRPC models with limited therapeutic options. The dataset includes ChIP-seq arms for TRIM24, STAT3, AR and H3K27ac in patient-derived xenograft and cell-line models. Why it matters for BB: this is the single most decision-ready refractory-cancer signal in days, because the reasoning layer does not sell the reader 'TRIM24 is a druggable target' (a database row) - it sells the structural insight that in refractory prostate cancer the DECISION-RELEVANT UNIT IS THE RESIDUAL-DISEASE STATE, not the primary driver, and that a target is only worth a degrader if it sustains the residual subpopulation that seeds the relapse. That reframes Brown Biotech's refractory-cancer lane from 'find a new target' to 'identify and deplete the relapse-primed residual population', which is exactly the clinical question clients actually ask. Two concrete payoffs for the AI-DD target-inference stack: (a) bi-functional (PROTAC-style) degradation is a modality the stack does not yet reason about at all - today it gets a citable, in-vivo-validated case where degradation beats inhibition because the target's role is survival-of-residual-disease rather than proliferation; (b) the STAT3 + EMT-cytokine arm shows that the escape mechanism runs through a signalling axis parallel to the drug's own target, which is a generalizable feature for scoring adaptive-resistance hypotheses in the multiomics service line. KEY mechanistic content, read from the depositor abstract and cross-read against the linked PubMed record (PMID 42784692): cellular senescence is a stress-induced program marked by durable cell-cycle arrest plus transcriptional and secretory changes that orchestrate tissue microenvironments. Although TRANSIENT SENESCENCE SUPPORTS TISSUE REPAIR, its CHRONIC PERSISTENCE drives fibrosis, immune suppression and tumor progression. The authors identify P-SELECTIN as a CONSERVED SURFACE MARKER of cells with senescent characteristics accumulating in murine AND human fibrotic tissues and in the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment of liver and lung cancers. Within this population, MACROPHAGES exhibit canonical senescence features coupled to programs promoting extracellular-matrix deposition and immune evasion. To selectively target the population they engineered P-SELECTIN-TARGETED SENESCENCE-MODULATING NANOPARTICLES (SMNPs) delivering senolytic or senomorphic agents. In preclinical fibrosis models, SMNPs eliminated or reprogrammed pathogenic cells, REVERSED ESTABLISHED FIBROSIS (not merely prevented its onset), and reduced free-drug-associated systemic toxicity. In cancer models, SMNPs remodeled the fibrotic microenvironment and markedly enhanced responses to immune checkpoint-based therapies. Why it matters for BB: this is the highest-value Longevity & Senolytics signal in days and it lands simultaneously in the fibrosis lane, which is Brown Biotech's named anti-fibrotic case-study territory.
