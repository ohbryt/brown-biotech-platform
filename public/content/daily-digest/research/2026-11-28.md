Why this one matters for BB: the entire AI-drug-discovery service lane in the archive has been about ranking single molecules against single targets. SINBA is a different business shape - it asks which PAIR of drugs, given that neither alone is sufficient, and it answers with a named pair and a named mechanism for why the pair is synergistic. The published numbers are unusually auditable for a computational framework: 32 combinations prioritised in silico, 19 experimentally validated as synergistic, so the hit rate is stated rather than implied, which is the single hardest thing to defend in a client brief about computational discovery. The disease framing is also precise rather than fashionable: Group 3 medulloblastoma, MYC-amplified, with limited druggable mutations and poor BBB penetration explicitly named as the reason standard approaches fail, which is a textbook refractory-cancer and drug-accessibility problem. The delivered pair (mirdametinib, a MEK inhibitor, with regorafenib, a p38 inhibitor) is clinically familiar chemistry rather than novel molecules, which makes it a cheap, fast test of the framework's value, and the add-on of low-dose radiation is the kind of combination-design detail that reads as a real-world plan. The single-cell arm adds the commercial part of the argument: the combination selectively eliminates the developmental cell-of-origin of Group 3 disease while reprogramming the immune microenvironment, so there is a patient-selection hypothesis attached, not just a drug list. Why it matters for BB: this is a named, public, methodologically-specified engine with a published validation rate and a worked clinical example - i.e. a template BB can put in front of a client as 'here is what a defensible computational combination-discovery workflow looks like, and here is the hit rate it achieved', which is far more persuasive than a generic model-accuracy slide. The honest caveats are substantial and are stated rather than glossed: the primary record is a bioRxiv preprint (DOI 10.1101/2025.11.20.689490, PMID 41332625) and not yet peer-reviewed, the framework requires disease-specific networks and curated drug-gene interaction data that are expensive to build per indication, the validation is preclinical in xenograft and immunocompetent mouse models rather than in patients, and the deposited GEO footprint is modest (n=4, n=8, n=17, n=1 across four accessions). A BB brief should treat this as a combination-discovery workflow template plus a named worked case, and should not treat the 19-of-32 validation rate as a transferable expectation without per-indication re-benchmarking.

### 2. [Signal 2 - Clinical and Environmental-Exposure Lane + Chronic Rhinosinusitis With Nasal Polyps Recognised as a Disease Driven by Epithelial Barrier Dysfunction in Response to Environmental Exposures + Mendelian Randomization Combined With Tissue-Level Exposure Profiling and Multi-Layered Mechanistic Validation Used to Delineate a Phthalate-Associated Pathogenic Axis + Di(2-Ethylhexyl) Phthalate Shown to Exacerbate Epithelial Remodeling Via IDO1-Mediated Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress + CXCR2/CCR1-Dependent Immune Cell Recruitment Identified as the Effector Arm of That Axis] GSE354996 'Di(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate exacerbates epithelial remodeling via IDO1-mediated endoplasmic reticulum stress and CXCR2/CCR1-dependent immune cell recruitment in chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps' (Homo sapiens, score 6, n=6 deposited libraries, pdat 2026/11/26, Expression profiling by high throughput sequencing, raw TXT, license NCBI public repository) is the clinical pick of the day and the first archive appearance of an EXPOSURE-DRIVEN DISEASE AXIS argued with causal-inference machinery rather than with association. The methodological spine is the interesting part, and it is rare in this archive: Mendelian randomization to establish that the exposure-to-outcome direction is not just correlated, tissue-level exposure profiling to show the agent actually reaches the tissue, and then multi-layered mechanistic validation to run the axis forward into an intervention point. That is a three-step argument that would satisfy a client's scientific advisor, and it is a template rather than a one-off. The mechanism it lands on is unusually actionable for a disease with no good drug: DEHP drives IDO1-mediated endoplasmic reticulum stress in the epithelium, and the resulting epithelial remodeling recruits immune cells through CXCR2 and CCR1 - meaning there are three separate intervention points (the enzyme, the stress pathway, the chemokine receptors) and the chemokine-receptor arm is a class with existing clinical precedent. Why this matters for BB: the commercial lane here is not CRSwNP drug discovery, it is the much larger and much better-funded lane of EXPOSURE-DRIVEN DISEASE MECHANISM - the same causal-inference-plus-tissue-profiling-plus-mechanism structure transfers directly to endocrine disruptors, particulates, microplastics, solvent and pesticide exposures, and to workplace or consumer-product safety dossiers, which is a market that pays for mechanism and evidence quality rather than for therapeutic novelty. The honest caveats: n=6 libraries is a small transcriptomic footprint, the human data are exposure-profiling plus MR rather than a prospective cohort, DEHP-specific findings do not generalise to other phthalates without separate work, and the GEO deposit does not itself carry the MR or validation experiments, so a brief can cite the mechanism but must source the causal-inference evidence from the paper.

[Signal 3 - Bioinformatics and Multi-Omics Lane + an Integrative Single-Cell Multi-Omics Analysis Uncovering the Regulatory Role of Siah2 in Controlling Metabolic Reprogramming and Inflammatory Signalling Within Mouse Bone Marrow-Derived Adipose Tissue Macrophages + Combined Genome-Binding and Expression Profiling Delivered as a Single H5 Multiome Object + the E3-Ligase Adaptor Nominated as a Metabolic-Inflammatory Control Point in Tissue-Resident Macrophage Populations] GSE355629 'Integrative analysis of single-cell multi-omics reveals a role for Siah2 in regulating metabolic and inflammatory pathways in mouse bone-marrow derived adipose tissue macrophages' (Mus musculus, score 4, n=8 deposited libraries, pdat 2026/11/26, Genome binding/occupancy profiling by high throughput sequencing plus Expression profiling by high throughput sequencing, raw H5, license NCBI public repository) is the bioinformatics pick of the day and the first archive appearance of a METABOLIC-INFLAMMATORY CONTROL POINT nominated by reading chromatin occupancy and expression JOINTLY at single-cell resolution. The methodological point is the one most single-cell papers get wrong: RNA alone tells you what a cell is expressing, and ATAC alone tells you what it could express, and the regulatory point only appears when both are read on the same cells - which is exactly what this deposit ships, as one H5 multiome object rather than two separate matrices. Siah2 is an E3-ligase adaptor, which makes it a drug-handle class (degrader-adjacent, E3-recruiting, target-degradation-capable) rather than a descriptive marker, and it sits at the intersection of adipose tissue macrophage metabolism and inflammatory signalling, which is the mechanistic ground of metabolic disease, insulin resistance and the chronic-low-grade-inflammation half of ageing. Why this matters for BB: a target nominated by joint occupancy-plus-expression analysis at single-cell resolution, in a macrophage population, on a metabolic-inflammation axis, is precisely the input shape the strict-omics service needs - and the availability of a clean raw H5 multiome object means a client brief can be backed by a re-analysable asset rather than by a figure. The honest caveats: n=8 libraries is a small footprint for a multiome experiment, the model is mouse bone-marrow-derived macrophages rather than human tissue-resident macrophages, the deposited data are the regulatory map rather than the perturbation pharmacology, and 'the regulatory role of Siah2' as stated by the depositor is an inference from the multiome integration rather than from a knockout or degrader experiment - which is the experiment a brief should specify next.
