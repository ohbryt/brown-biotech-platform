## 🎯 Highlights

### 1. [Signal 1 - DASIT turns antigen presence into a selectable signal at the protein level, decoupling selection from vector integration: GSE349888 (Mus musculus, score 4, n=12, pdat 2026/11/09 = FRESHEST NOVEL accession band of the day, raw CSV): the AI-drug-discovery and biotech-infrastructure pick of the day, and the first NOVEL selectable-cell-circuit dataset in the archive. The framing problem is stated plainly in the depositor summary and it is a problem every BB client who runs a screen eventually hits: efficient and scalable isolation of specific cell populations remains a central bottleneck for genome engineering, pooled screening and cell-therapy manufacturing. The mechanism is the interesting part and it is elegant rather than clever - a destabilized nanobody fused to an antibiotic-resistance protein means that binding of the nanobody fusion to its cognate antigen stabilises the whole circuit, so antigen presence is converted directly into a selectable signal; the circuits were built to target distinct antigen classes and to work across cell types, and because the architecture operates at the protein level it supports both stable integration and transient delivery, which yields recyclable selection with no permanent genomic integration of resistance markers. The three demonstrations are deliberately the hard cases rather than the easy ones: multiplexed, logic-gated integration of landing pads in human iPSCs, high-throughput CRISPR screening, and phenotypic selection of in vitro-derived neurons at transplantation scale - all FACS-free. Why it matters for BB: this is an infrastructure asset, not a target list, and it is exactly the kind of asset BB's AI drug discovery and automation briefs keep being asked to specify - a selection method that is automation-compatible by construction, that scales to library-scale screens without FACS, and that can be run transiently so selection reagents do not contaminate the downstream manufacturing step. It also changes the economics of pooled screening: logic-gated selection with recyclable markers is the kind of design that a client would otherwise have to invent. Two cautions: the sequence arm is Mus musculus with n=12 and the paper's own claim is that the mechanism works across cell types, so a client brief should treat the human iPSC landing-pad application as the headline rather than generalising to primary human cells without evidence; and none of today's three records lists a linked peer-reviewed identifier, so this sits at the depositor-abstract stage and should be presented as a platform design with strong internal validation rather than as a validated product.

### 2. [Signal 2 - DDX17 is a post-transcriptional rheostat of macrophage inflammatory tone, with Stat2 as the direct RNA target and an in vivo conditional knockout readout: GSE331179 with the companion GSE331060 seCLIP arm (Mus musculus, score 4, n=12 and n=4, pdat 2026/11/08, raw BW + TXT + BED): the target-nomination pick of the day and the first NOVEL RNA-helicase-interactome dataset in the archive. The technical core is a specific method choice with a specific reason: because DDX17's direct RNA targets in primary immune cells had been hard to resolve, the authors used single-end enhanced CLIP (seCLIP), a streamlined crosslinking and immunoprecipitation approach that maps direct protein-RNA interactions at nucleotide resolution, and applied it to primary bone-marrow-derived macrophages. The result is a genuine mechanism rather than a correlation: DDX17 preferentially bound transcripts governing RNA processing and innate immune signalling, with Stat2 identified as a prominent target; mice with myeloid-specific deletion of DDX17 (using a newly generated conditional allele) exhibited elevated Stat2 expression, amplified interferon signalling and splenomegaly, showing that macrophage DDX17 restrains immune activation in vivo; and myeloid DDX17 deficiency reshaped inflammatory responses within the vessel wall, as revealed by single-cell RNA-seq, without altering atherosclerosis lesion size. That last clause is the commercially sharp part - it means the intervention changes inflammatory phenotype without changing plaque burden, i.e. it is a target for modulating the inflammatory tone of an existing lesion rather than for preventing the lesion, which is a materially different indication and a materially different client conversation. Why it matters for BB: this is the shape BB's AI drug discovery lane sells - a named RNA-binding protein, its direct RNA target resolved at nucleotide resolution, a conditional in vivo allele, and a phenotype decoupled from disease burden - and DDX17 is itself described in the depositor summary as a DEAD-box helicase already implicated in cancer and antiviral immunity, so the same node supports at least two indication lanes. Two cautions: the causal chain runs through interferon signalling and Stat2 elevation, which is a broad axis with known antiviral-function liabilities, so a brief must address whether damping interferon tone in macrophages is a tolerable risk in an immunocompromised or vaccinated patient; and the seCLIP arm is n=4 with the wider scRNA-seq at n=12, so the lesion-burden decoupling needs independent replication before it is used to argue an indication.
