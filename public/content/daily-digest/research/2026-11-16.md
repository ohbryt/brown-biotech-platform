## 🎯 Highlights

### 1. [Signal 1 - Anti-Fibrotic and Liver-Fibrosis Lane + MAZ as the Transcriptional Gate on Hepatic Fatty-Acid Uptake + FABP1 as the Executable Effector Node + Dihydroartemisinin as an In-Hand Proof That the Axis Is Druggable] GSE353867 'MAZ drives MASLD progression through promoting FABP1-mediated fatty acid uptake [HepG2_cell_lines_RNA-seq]' with companion mouse arm GSE353868 (Homo sapiens, score 6, n=6, plus Mus musculus, score 4, n=12, pdat 2026/11/14 = FRESHEST NOVEL accession band of the day, raw CSV, license clean) is the anti-fibrotic pick of the day and the first NOVEL MAZ-FABP1 record in the archive. The clinical framing is the commercially crowded MASLD-to-HCC axis, and the study enters it from the transcription-factor side rather than from another lipid or metabolite. MAZ (Myc-associated zinc finger protein) is elevated in MASLD patient liver, in four independent MASLD mouse models (ob/ob, Western diet, CDAHFD, GAN) and across three FFA-challenged cellular systems (mouse primary hepatocytes, HepG2, THLE-2). The functional direction is bidirectional and therefore mechanistically credible: MAZ knockdown attenuates lipid accumulation in all three cellular models and ameliorates hepatic steatosis, lipid accumulation, hyperlipidemia, inflammation, oxidative stress, fibrosis and systemic metabolic disturbance in MASLD mice, while MAZ overexpression worsens MASLD progression in CDAHFD-fed mice. The mechanism is resolved to a specific promoter: MAZ directly binds the FABP1 promoter to activate transcription, thereby promoting hepatic fatty-acid uptake; Fabp1 knockdown attenuates the deleterious effect of Maz overexpression; and FABP1 overexpression rescues the fatty-acid-uptake and lipid-accumulation defect caused by MAZ knockdown - a closed bidirectional epistasis, which is the design feature that separates this from a correlative fatty-acid study. The commercially decisive clause is the last one: dihydroartemisinin, a derivative of artemisinin, is used as a tool to verify MAZ as a therapeutic target for MASLD, reducing MAZ and FABP1 expression and decreasing FABP1 promoter activity. Why it matters for BB: this is one of the rare public MASLD deposits that arrives with a named pharmacologic probe already applied to the nominated axis, which means the client conversation can be 'MAZ is the gate, FABP1 is the effector, DHA is the tool compound that proves the axis is reachable' rather than 'here is another lipid-gene correlation'. The MAZ-FABP1 axis also plugs directly into BB's existing MASLD and liver-fibrosis programme surface (the Sargassum japonica and MASH briefs), because the fibrosis readouts sit downstream of the same node, so the same perturbation arm yields both metabolic and anti-fibrotic pharmacodynamic markers. Two cautions: the tool compound is a verification probe rather than a developed clinical asset and its own therapeutic index and selectivity against MAZ are not established by this deposit, and the human evidence is expression-level in patient liver samples with no human interventional arm - so the honest framing is 'genetically validated in four mouse models and three human systems, pharmacologically probed in a cell model', not 'clinical validation'.

### 2. [Signal 2 - AI-Drug-Discovery and Gene-Therapy Lane + Desmoplakin Haploinsufficiency Identified as the Primary Defect in DSP Cardiomyopathy Rather Than a Filament-Structure Defect + Contractile Force Identified as the Exposure Variable That Triggers Adhesion Failure + Mavacamten as a Pharmacologic Proof of the Causal Chain + CRISPR Transcriptional Activation as the Size-Circumventing Therapeutic Route] GSE353951 'Desmoplakin Haploinsufficiency Underlies Cell-Cell Adhesion Failure in DSP Cardiomyopathy and is Rescued by Transcriptional Activation' with companion human-heart arm GSE353952 (Homo sapiens, score 6, n=25 and n=17, pdat 2026/11/14, raw CSV, license clean) is the AI-drug-discovery pick of the day and the first NOVEL desmoplakin-haploinsufficiency record in the archive. The unmet need is stated bluntly by the depositors: truncating variants in desmoplakin are a leading cause of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy, a severe heart-muscle disease characterised by fibrosis and life-threatening arrhythmias; DSP anchors the junctions that hold heart-muscle cells together to the internal cytoskeleton so the tissue can withstand the force of each contraction; no targeted therapy exists; and DSP is too large for conventional gene replacement. The reframe is the interesting part. Rather than treating the mutation as a broken-filament problem, the study shows in explanted human hearts and hiPSC-derived cardiomyocytes that truncating variants reduce DSP RNA and protein, and that the resulting haploinsufficiency is what renders cardiomyocytes vulnerable to cell-cell adhesion failure under contractile stress, with membrane disruption preceding the adhesion failure. Two functional arms close the causal chain rather than merely describing it: lowering contractile force with the myosin inhibitor mavacamten prevents the failure, which makes mechanical load an identified and modulable exposure variable; and CRISPR-based transcriptional activation of the endogenous gene circumvents the gene-size limitation, restores desmoplakin expression and rescues adhesion, establishing haploinsufficiency as the primary defect and transcriptional activation as a therapeutic strategy in its own right. Why it matters for BB: this is a target-validation package that arrives with three things most client briefs have to be paid to build - a defined causal defect (haploinsufficiency, not loss of a structural domain), a pharmacologic probe already in clinical use (mavacamten) that validates the exposure variable, and a modality-matched rescue route (CRISPRa) for a gene too large for AAV replacement. That combination is what turns a cardiomyopathy finding into a credible platform proposition for the AI-drug-discovery lane, because the deliverable is not a candidate molecule but a dosing and biomarker strategy around restoring transcript dosage in a load-dependent tissue. It also gives the refractory-cancer lane a transferable pattern: identify the minimal protein dosage threshold, then choose between load reduction and transcriptional upregulation. Cautions: the cardiomyocyte model is hiPSC-derived and human hearts are explanted rather than functional outcomes, so the durability of transcriptional activation and the arrhythmogenic risk of over-restoring DSP are both open; and CRISPRa delivery to myocardium is a delivery challenge the deposit does not solve.

### 3. [Signal 3 - Epitranscriptomic Target-Nomination Lane + Viral RNA Capture Within the First Hour of Infection as a Systematic Host-Dependency Screen + 146 Human Proteins Directly Bound to the Incoming Viral Genome + SPEN, RBM15 and RBM15B Recast from Xist Regulators to Proviral Host Factors + SPEN Depletion Broadly Remodelling Viral m6A With Loss Concentrated in N- and P-Encoding mRNAs] GSE354350 'Early Host-Virus RNA Interactions Reveal SPEN-Driven m6A Regulation as a Major Determinant of Henipavirus Infection' (Homo sapiens, score 6, n=8, pdat 2026/11/14, gds_type Expression profiling by high throughput sequencing; Methylation profiling by high throughput sequencing, raw CSV, license clean) is the third NOVEL signal and the one with the most reusable platform value. The method is the point: VIR-CLASP (Viral Cross-linking and Solid-phase Purification) captures host proteins bound to the incoming henipavirus genome within the first hour of infection, which is a systematic, timing-anchored host-dependency screen rather than a downstream infectivity assay. The output is a henipavirus RNA-host protein interactome of 146 human proteins directly associated with the primary viral RNA, and the standout finding is that SPEN, RBM15 and RBM15B - the canonical regulators of lncRNA Xist, previously associated mainly with X-chromosome inactivation - act as key proviral host factors. Direct RNA sequencing then shows that SPEN depletion broadly remodels viral m6A, with pronounced modification losses concentrated within N- and P-encoding mRNAs, and the authors frame the whole result as exposing a critical layer of host dependency at the very onset of infection and an unappreciated role for SPEN family proteins in driving infection through selective viral epitranscriptomic control. Why it matters for BB: two commercially relevant angles, one of them deliberately non-obvious. First, as a target-discovery platform, VIR-CLASP is a generalisable method that turns any high-consequence viral genome into a list of human host proteins that bind it, and it is directly reusable for BB's pathogen-preparedness and host-directed-therapy briefs - and the 146-protein interactome is a prioritised panel rather than an unwielded list, with SPEN-family members at the top because they are established, drug-able RNA-binding proteins with in-hand tool chemistry. Second, the Xist connection is the striking claim: SPEN family proteins are best known as suppressors of X-linked gene expression, and here the same proteins are repurposed by the virus as licensing factors for its own transcriptome - which reframes the family from a dosage-sensitive epigenetic regulator to an anti-viral liability, and makes any existing SPEN inhibitor or degrader a dual-use asset. The N/P concentration is also mechanistically sharp because N and P are the henipavirus polymerase and accessory proteins, so modification loss on those transcripts is loss of the replication engine's own expression, which is a much stronger causal story than diffuse viral hypomethylation. Cautions: this is BSL-4 pathogen work and the deposit is a host-dependency map rather than a therapeutic demonstration - there is no inhibitor arm here, and the SPEN-depletion arm is loss-of-function rather than pharmacology; also 2026's archive has already carried one epitranscriptomics signal (the YB-1 m5C reader in the 2026-11-13 entry), so the novelty here is the SPEN host-dependency angle and the method, not epitranscriptomics as a category.
