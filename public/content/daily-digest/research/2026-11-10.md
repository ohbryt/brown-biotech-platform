## 🎯 Highlights

### 1. [Signal 1 - StateMap turns a pathway from a binary label into a five-state continuum and nominates an adhesion dependency with an approved-name inhibitor: GSE353663 (Homo sapiens, score 9, n=6, pdat 2026/11/08 = FRESHEST NOVEL accession band of the day, raw CSV): the AI-drug-discovery pick of the day and the first NOVEL pathway-state-reconstruction dataset in the archive. The framing problem it solves is one that sits squarely in BB's AI drug discovery lane: transcriptomic signatures incompletely capture pathway activity while CRISPR dependency profiles lack mechanistic context, so the two data types have to be integrated before a dependency can be interpreted. StateMap does exactly that - it reconstructs pathway states by integrating CRISPR fitness dependencies with the associated transcriptional programs - and applied to the Hippo pathway it resolves a continuum into five interpretable regions rather than the conventional YAP-on/YAP-off classification. The finding that matters commercially is the Hippo-strong state, defined by strong growth-restrictive effects of Hippo pathway genes DESPITE active YAP/TAZ signalling; these cells selectively depended on integrin alphaVbeta5, ITGAV depletion induced Hippo-dependent cell aggregation followed by G1 arrest, projection onto tumour transcriptomes nominated a corresponding subset with elevated ITGB1/NNMT expression, and drug-response datasets plus validation assays showed increased sensitivity of Hippo-strong models to the alphaV integrin inhibitor cilengitide. Why it matters for BB: this is a whole-discovery-workflow asset rather than a target list - it is a reusable computational layer that converts a dependency map plus a transcriptome into discrete, drug-testable states, and it arrives with a validation ladder already attached (projection to tumour transcriptomes, drug-response data, wet-lab validation) and a commercially named drug at the end of it. That makes it a template for the paid AI drug discovery brief: state definition, dependency nominated, perturbation applied, drug sensitivity shown. Two cautions: n=6 human samples in the sequencing arm makes it a mechanism template rather than a cohort, and the Hippo-strong fraction is a minority state, so a client-facing brief must state enrichment prevalence rather than imply pan-tumour applicability; and the archive's prior AI-discovery entries have all been gene-level, so the differentiation is specifically state-level dependency nomination.

The dataset resolves the mechanism with matched human tissue rather than with a model system: Nanostring, scRNA-seq and high-dimensional immunofluorescence multiplexing of matched perifocal and focal tissue from n=15 DRE patients revealed a unique microglia polarization state with elevated diazepam binding inhibitor (DBI, log2 FC 1.45); ex vivo culture of human tissue confirmed higher DBI secretion from epileptogenic foci relative to perifocal tissue (85.72 ng/ml versus 55.67 ng/ml, P < 0.0001); and single-cell spatial transcriptomics on Visium HD in minimal-residual-disease glioblastoma samples (n=4), combined with scRNA-seq (n=110), showed robust DBI expression correlating with glioblastoma progression and tumour connectivity scores. ### 3. [Signal 3 - IgD geometry, not IgD sequence, sets tumour-cell binding: GSE349653 (Homo sapiens, score 6, n=12, pdat 2026/11/08, raw TAR + CSV): the antibody-discovery pick of the day and the first NOVEL IgD-avidity dataset in the archive. The biology of the cell population is unusual and worth stating first: Immunoglobulin D is primarily a B-cell receptor on naive B cells and secreted IgD is rare, so the authors went looking in non-small cell lung cancer and identified a rare population of IgD-secreting plasma cells exhibiting extensive somatic hypermutation, clonal expansion and lineage relationships consistent with germinal-centre-dependent differentiation - in other words, these are antigen-experienced, clonally expanded effectors, not a contamination artefact. The finding is then isolated experimentally rather than asserted: patient-derived monoclonal antibodies showed markedly greater tumour-cell binding when expressed with the IgD Fab2 region than their IgG1 or IgM counterparts carrying identical variable regions, and because monomeric IgD showed no enhanced monovalent or bivalent binding kinetics, the unique structural properties of IgD do not account for the increase - instead the IgD Fab2 region promotes the formation of reversible tetramers with potential octavalency, thereby increasing avidity, enhancing polyreactivity and promoting binding to tumour cells and to unrelated recombinant complex antigens. Why it matters for BB: this is a structure-is-the-mechanism result, and structure-based antibody engineering is exactly the commercial question clients ask when an IgG candidate underperforms - it says that affinity is not the only lever and that a reversible tetramer-forming isotype can raise effective valency without changing the variable region, which reframes the target-engagement problem as an architecture problem. It also supplies a patient-derived, tumour-binding antibody repertoire with documented somatic hypermutation, which is a rare public input for antibody-discovery briefs. Two cautions: polyreactivity is a double-edged property that raises the risk of off-target binding and immunogenicity, so a client brief should treat it as a liability to be engineered out rather than purely as an advantage; and n=12 samples with a limited patient-derived panel is a screening set, not a developability package.
